Since 1847

A frontier town built by a soldier, shaped by its people.

Jacobabad stands on the site of a small village once called Khangarh, in the far north of Sindh where the province meets Balochistan. It takes its name from Brigadier‑General John Jacob, an officer of the British East India Company who served as the first deputy commissioner of the Upper Sindh Frontier and made the town his headquarters.

Jacob is remembered less for conquest than for construction — roads, a cantonment, and irrigation works including the canal system that turned the surrounding desert into farmland. The town was incorporated as a municipality in 1875 and grew into the administrative and commercial heart of the district that still bears his name.

Today Jacobabad sits at a crossroads of national highways and rail lines, a market town for rice and grain, known equally for its Sindhi hospitality, its handicrafts — lacquered wood, embroidery, carpets — and for holding the record for the highest temperature ever recorded in Pakistan.

  • 1847General John Jacob establishes his headquarters at the village of Khangarh, laying out the modern town.
  • 1875Jacobabad is formally incorporated as a municipality under British administration.
  • Pre‑1947Serves as the administrative headquarters of the Upper Sindh Frontier District, Bombay Presidency.
  • 1947 →Becomes part of Pakistan at Partition; grows into a key transport and trade hub of northern Sindh.
  • TodayCapital of Jacobabad District — home to markets, shrines, colonial‑era architecture and a busy rail junction.

Jacobabad Railway Station

The city's rail junction (station code JCD) — trains, palm trees and a dusty pink dusk sky, with the bilingual "Jacobabad Junction" sign that greets every arrival.

Tehsil 07

Thull Tehsil

The district's largest tehsil by population — a farming region linked by road and rail to Kandhkot, Shikarpur and the Balochistan border.
